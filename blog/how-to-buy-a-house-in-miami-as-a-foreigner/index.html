How to Buy a House in Miami as a Foreigner: Guide & Requirements

Learn how foreign buyers can purchase a home in Miami, what documents are commonly requested, financing options and the main steps to closing.

If you are considering a real estate investment in South Florida, you may wonder whether you can buy a house in Miami as a foreigner. In general, foreign nationals can purchase U.S. real estate without being U.S. citizens or permanent residents. The transaction, however, should be planned carefully because financing, taxes, ownership structure and documentation may differ from a domestic purchase.

Can a foreigner legally buy property in Miami?

Foreign ownership of real estate is broadly permitted in Florida, subject to applicable federal and state rules. A buyer does not normally need a Green Card or U.S. citizenship simply to hold title to a residential property. Buyers should still confirm whether any specific law applies to their nationality, property type or location.

Many international transactions can also be coordinated remotely. Electronic signatures, remote document review and title-company procedures may reduce the need to be physically present for every stage.

Documents commonly requested from foreign buyers

Requirements vary by transaction and by lender, but international buyers are often asked for:

  • Valid passport or other accepted government identification.
  • Proof of funds showing the resources available for the deposit, purchase price and closing costs.
  • Bank references or account history, especially when financing is involved.
  • Income documentation from an employer, accountant or business records.
  • U.S. tax identification when required for tax reporting or income-producing property.

Can foreign buyers get a mortgage in Florida?

Yes. Some U.S. lenders offer programs designed for foreign nationals. Instead of relying only on a U.S. credit score, lenders may evaluate international bank statements, income, liquid assets, rental income and the property itself.

Typical considerations

  • Down payments are often higher than for domestic borrowers.
  • Lenders may require several months of reserves after closing.
  • Interest rates and fees may differ from standard resident mortgage programs.
  • Some investor loans focus on property cash flow, such as DSCR-based financing.

Terms change frequently, so buyers should obtain current quotes from lenders that work with international clients before making an offer that depends on financing.

Should you buy personally or through an LLC?

Some international investors use a U.S. limited liability company, while others buy in their personal name or through a different structure. The right answer depends on liability, tax residency, estate planning, financing and the intended use of the property.

An LLC can offer liability separation in appropriate situations, but it does not automatically reduce taxes and it may create additional filing obligations. A cross-border tax professional or attorney should review the structure before the contract is signed.

Step-by-step purchase process

  1. Define the objective. Decide whether the property is for personal use, long-term rental, short-term rental or appreciation.
  2. Set the budget and financing plan. Obtain proof of funds or mortgage pre-approval.
  3. Select the area and property. Compare pricing, carrying costs, rental rules and local demand.
  4. Submit an offer. The contract should clearly address deposits, inspection and financing contingencies when applicable.
  5. Complete due diligence. Inspection and title review help identify physical or legal issues before closing.
  6. Close the transaction. Funds are transferred through the title or escrow process and the deed is recorded.

Plan the investment before selecting the listing

Buying a home in Miami as a foreigner can be straightforward when the transaction is organized from the beginning.
