Mortgage Financing for Foreign Buyers in Miami

Learn how foreign-national mortgage programs work in Miami, including typical down payments, documentation, reserves and loan options for investors.

A common misconception is that international buyers must pay 100% cash for property in South Florida. In reality, specialized lenders offer mortgage financing for foreign buyers in Miami, allowing qualified investors to preserve liquidity while acquiring U.S. real estate.

What is a foreign-national mortgage?

A foreign-national mortgage is a loan designed for borrowers who are not U.S. citizens or permanent residents and may not have a traditional U.S. credit history. The lender typically evaluates the borrower's international financial profile, available assets, income and the characteristics of the property.

Typical down payment and loan-to-value

Foreign buyers generally provide more equity than domestic owner-occupants. Depending on the program, property type and borrower profile, the lender may finance only a portion of the purchase price and require a substantial down payment.

The exact percentages change by lender and market conditions, so buyers should treat any published range as a planning estimate rather than a guaranteed term.

Documents lenders may request

  • Valid passport and secondary identification when required.
  • Recent personal or business bank statements.
  • Evidence of income from employment, business ownership or investments.
  • Reference letters from financial institutions.
  • Purchase contract and property information.
  • Evidence of funds for the down payment, closing costs and post-closing reserves.

What if you do not have a U.S. credit score?

Many foreign-national programs are designed specifically for borrowers without a FICO score. A lender may use alternative credit references, asset verification or the income generated by the property. This is one reason it is important to work with a lender that regularly handles international borrowers rather than assuming a conventional domestic mortgage will fit.

DSCR loans for investment property

Debt Service Coverage Ratio loans are frequently discussed by real estate investors because underwriting focuses heavily on whether expected property income can support the debt payment. They can be useful for certain rental strategies, but the lender still reviews the property, reserves, borrower profile and program rules.

Fixed-rate and adjustable-rate options

Depending on the lender, foreign buyers may find fixed-rate, adjustable-rate or other portfolio loan structures. The best choice depends on the intended holding period, expected cash flow and risk tolerance.

Costs beyond the down payment

International buyers should budget for more than the purchase price. Closing costs may include lender fees, appraisal, title services, recording, prepaid insurance, property taxes and association-related charges. Investors should also model ongoing insurance, HOA, maintenance and management expenses.

How to prepare before making an offer

  1. Organize bank and income documentation from your home country.
  2. Speak with a foreign-national lender before selecting a property.
  3. Request a written estimate of the down payment, rate, fees and reserves.
  4. Confirm that the target property is eligible for the intended loan.
  5. Make the purchase contract consistent with your financing timeline.

Use financing as part of the investment strategy

Mortgage financing can help international investors preserve cash and diversify capital, but leverage also increases monthly obligations and financial risk. Inversiones Davar can help you evaluate properties while specialized lenders determine the financing structure that fits your profile.
