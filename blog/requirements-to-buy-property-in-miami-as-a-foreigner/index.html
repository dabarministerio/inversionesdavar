Requirements to Buy Property in Miami as a Foreigner

Review the common requirements for foreign buyers purchasing property in Miami, including identification, proof of funds, financing and tax considerations.

South Florida remains a major destination for international real estate buyers. Understanding the requirements to buy property in Miami as a foreigner can make the transaction more predictable and help you prepare the right documents before you begin negotiating.

Do you need U.S. residency to buy real estate?

In general, a foreign buyer does not need U.S. citizenship or permanent residency simply to acquire a home, condominium or investment property in Florida. Other legal restrictions can apply in specific circumstances, so buyers should verify current rules with qualified counsel when necessary.

Basic requirements for a cash purchase

A cash transaction is usually simpler because there is no mortgage underwriting. Commonly requested items include:

  • Passport or government identification for every person or entity taking title.
  • Proof of funds such as recent bank statements showing sufficient liquidity.
  • Source-of-funds documentation if requested by the title company, bank or other regulated party.
  • Contact and address information for the buyer.
  • Tax identification information when required for future tax filings or rental income.

Additional requirements when using mortgage financing

Foreign-national mortgage programs can require a broader financial file than a cash purchase.

Down payment

International buyers are often asked to contribute a larger down payment than U.S. resident borrowers. The exact amount depends on the lender, property type, loan size and borrower profile.

Income and asset verification

Lenders may request employer letters, accountant-prepared income statements, bank statements, investment statements or business documentation from the buyer's country of residence.

Banking references and reserves

Some programs request bank-reference letters and evidence that the borrower will maintain several months of mortgage, tax, insurance and association payments after closing.

Ownership structure and tax planning

Documentation is only one part of the purchase. International buyers should also decide how title will be held. Personal ownership, an LLC or another entity can produce different liability, financing, estate-planning and tax consequences.

Using an LLC

An LLC may be useful for liability separation and business administration, particularly for investment property, but it is not a universal solution. Entity classification and tax treatment should be reviewed by a professional familiar with cross-border ownership.

ITIN and U.S. tax reporting

An Individual Taxpayer Identification Number may become necessary for certain federal tax filings, especially when a foreign owner earns U.S. rental income or later sells the property.

FIRPTA when selling

Foreign sellers of U.S. real property may be subject to FIRPTA withholding at the time of sale. Withholding is generally an advance collection mechanism rather than the final tax calculation. Planning before the sale can help avoid surprises at closing.

Purchase process checklist

  1. Define the investment objective and target area.
  2. Determine whether the purchase will be cash or financed.
  3. Confirm the intended ownership structure before signing when possible.
  4. Submit an offer and fund the escrow deposit according to the contract.
  5. Complete inspection and title due diligence.
  6. Satisfy lender conditions, if any.
  7. Review closing documents and transfer the final funds.
  8. Record the deed and organize post-closing tax and property-management obligations.
